# The Most Underrated MRR Drop-servicing Niches to hit $20k/mo

- of: [src.md](src.md)
- digested by: /ingest digest, by opus, 2026-11-21

## gist

Eric, who runs remote service arbitrage on Google Business Profiles with no ads, usually on high-ticket
one-off emergency trades (plumbing, HVAC, electrical), lays out three monthly recurring niches instead:
pool cleaning (Dallas: the one keyworded profile ranks #1 on 26 reviews over 453; instant verification;
~$80 a weekly visit; needs a warm city), pest control (Dallas: ~20,000 searches/mo, recurring plans plus
emergency jobs, verifiable without video, more competitive and seasonal; "100k a month in profit" in
Dallas alone, his estimate) and commercial cleaning (no seasonality, janitorial service instant-verifies,
clients hard to leave, a construction cleanout pays a couple thousand, longer sales cycles; Chicago's
restaurant cleaning, 4,400 searches/mo, is held by one operator's profiles at 14–23 reviews). Recurring
trades take more calls and a VA earlier than plumbing. Going narrower inside a trade, to a sub-service
with its own volume, ranks you first where the broad term would not.

### [00:03:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r_6NXlQzHJ0&t=228) pool cleaning: low tickets, more management, warm cities only

- the cons: lower ticket sizes and margins. After paying the person who does the job there is little
  profit per job; it rests on customers recurring and adding up: three customers the first week make
  $120 that week, the same three plus three more make $240 the next, and so on
  <!--says 1σ ?t=228-->

- more management: more people to handle means more skin in the game, more VA training, and a VA hired
  before you make 20–30k a month. Plumbing can reach the $30k/mo mark on five calls a day; pool cleaning
  needs a lot more phone calls
  <!--says 1σ ?t=260-->

- seasonal outside warm places: not feasible in a super cold country or city, good clients in summer and
  dry in winter. Find a warm area; Dallas is the example he gives away
