## gist

In this video, I walk through the system I use to get GMB pages verified instantly or by email
code, instead of leaving it purely to luck. I cover what each method requires, how to set up a
domain email for the code, how to submit a listing and enter the code, how paid storage and an
aged account raise the odds of instant verification, and how to get other people to run the
verification flow on their own phones. I show two live examples of profiles getting instantly
verified, then close with the success rates and the trade-off that these listings get suspended
faster than video-verified ones.

### [02:22](https://www.loom.com/share/044f7bdb93d14d329947ed50f9391dd4?t=142) Setting Up a Domain Email for Verification

- Uses Hostinger (~$20/month) for hosting because it includes free email addresses on each domain.
  <!--ran 2σ ?t=142-->
- Cheap AI-built websites and $1/year `.site` domains are good enough, since verification doesn't check domain quality.
  <!--says 1σ ?t=142-->
- Creates a custom email alias (e.g. eric@domain.com) in the hosting mailbox panel and logs into it in a browser to receive the code.
  <!--ran 2σ ?t=142-->

### [05:04](https://www.loom.com/share/044f7bdb93d14d329947ed50f9391dd4?t=304) Submitting the Listing and Entering the Email Code

- Always fills out the GMB listing form in incognito/private browsing on cellular data, never repeatedly on the same wifi.
  <!--ran 2σ ?t=304-->
- Gets a tracking number from CallRail but waits to purchase it until after the page verifies, to avoid wasting money on unverified attempts.
  <!--ran 2σ ?t=304-->
- Enters the domain email to trigger a code, retrieves it from the mailbox, and submits it.
  <!--ran 2σ ?t=304-->
- Verification or suspension usually shows within a couple minutes, though it can take up to five days; estimates roughly 1 in 5 email-code attempts succeed, at about $3 per Gmail account tried.
  <!--ran 2σ ?t=304-->

### [10:20](https://www.loom.com/share/044f7bdb93d14d329947ed50f9391dd4?t=620) Instant Verification: Trust Signals and Other People's Phones

- Instant verification is mostly luck, but improves with a high local guide level, purchased Google storage (the rainbow profile ring), and an aged account.
  <!--says 1σ ?t=620-->
- As an alternative to buying aged accounts, pays other people, e.g. a friend's younger brother, to run the verification flow on their own phones at places like the mall.
  <!--ran 2σ ?t=620-->
- Builds rapport and approaches people around his own age, since Google allows only one attempt per phone.
  <!--ran 2σ ?t=620-->

### [13:32](https://www.loom.com/share/044f7bdb93d14d329947ed50f9391dd4?t=812) Recap: Odds, Requirements, and Downsides

- Instant verification succeeds roughly 1 in 7 attempts; the key factors are incognito plus cellular data, aged (3+ year) Gmail accounts, and using real local people's phones.
  <!--says 1σ ?t=812-->
- These listings get suspended faster than video-verified ones, often lasting one to two months, but the low time and money cost still makes it worth doing in volume.
  <!--ran 2σ ?t=812-->
- Suggests using address pages far from your actual location, since no on-site video is required, and falling back to the email code method if instant verification doesn't go through.
