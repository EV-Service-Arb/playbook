# Mastering the Money-Making Model for Local Services

## gist

Eric's short intro to the model: a searcher looks up a service in an area on Google Search or Maps, and a list of companies shows after the sponsored ads. Address pages rank better than service-area pages because of proximity, but they are harder to put up and more likely to be suspended.

## chapters

### [00:00](https://www.loom.com/share/1f32c7640a514b198adc997ca319238b?t=0) The model, and address pages vs service-area pages

- the model: someone searches a service plus an area ("Plumber near me", "Plumber Markham") and a list of companies appears after the sponsored ads
  <!--says ?t=0-->
- Eric owns "Plumber Done Right in Markham", listed on an address, while a competitor (Everest Drain and Plumbing) is a service-area page with no address
  <!--says ?t=0-->
- service-area pages are "a lot less risky but the results are not typically as good"; "addresses are always going to be superior if you can nail them down properly", but they are harder to verify and "way, way more likely to get suspended"
  <!--says ?t=0-->

### [02:02](https://www.loom.com/share/1f32c7640a514b198adc997ca319238b?t=122) Why an address ranks: proximity

- proximity: a pizza search shows the closest Domino's, not the one 20 km away; so people near the listed address are far more likely to see you in their top three "even if your profile has poorly done SEO, even if it has poor pictures"
  <!--says ?t=122-->
- people call the first, second or third result "90% of the time", so with no other plumber nearby a weak profile on an address still shows up first for them
