# Avoiding Suspensions: Best Practices for Your GMB

## gist

Eric lists roughly six things that influence whether a page is suspended: name and keywords, edits, access, speed, the address, and how it was created. The first three he calls sure things; the last he says are anecdote. He grants that on addresses you do not own, "black hat", some suspension cannot be avoided.

## chapters

### [00:00](https://www.loom.com/share/088937bc44854962b7145d022e98184a?t=0) Name and keywords

- no keyword stuffing: Google "hates" a page that looks botted. Do not put the service next to the city in the name (`Done Right Plumber Ajax` is "a huge flag"): possible, but someone reports it and Google is "really strict"
  <!--Eric says ?t=0-->
- a report from a Local Guide account, or a suggested edit by one, gets the page suspended
  <!--Eric says ?t=0-->
- in the description the keywords go in as a customer would say them ("Plumbers near me in Ajax"), not as a list ("Plumber near me, Plumber Ajax, drain problem, drain clog")
  <!--Eric says ?t=0-->

### [03:57](https://www.loom.com/share/088937bc44854962b7145d022e98184a?t=237) Edits, access

- never edit the address ("instant suspension ... most of the time"), website, phone number, name, category or business location. Everything else is fine, but one at a time: a description and a couple photos on day one, service areas (about five) and two or three products the next. He has made all edits at once and not been suspended, "it is possible"
  <!--Eric says ?t=237-->
- do not give anyone owner access; send yourself manager access. It comes up when they work on other people's phones with instant approvals
  <!--Eric ran ?t=237-->
- not several GMBs on one account: he has some, but is "safer not doing that". Buy 5 to 10 aged Gmail accounts, about $15, or use your personal emails; he does not see why same-account pages would be banned
  <!--Eric says ?t=237-->

### [07:28](https://www.loom.com/share/088937bc44854962b7145d022e98184a?t=448) Speed, address, creation

- once a page is up, do not touch it for the first five days and try not to get reviews in them. After, ramp up: a couple of reviews on day one, 20 on the next; "it doesn't really matter", since a thousand-person text blast giving 50 reviews is realistic
  <!--Eric says ?t=448-->
- address: addresses that stay up are commercial buildings where nobody "can confirm or deny" a plumbing office, with other GMBs listed there, and a unit number "has helped a lot". A house is the contrary: there is plainly no office. Eric calls this "bro math", unsure it matters
  <!--Eric says ?t=448-->
- creation: on a Gmail account with no existing GMB, an aged one if possible. The same account can be used for several business videos, but he prefers not
  <!--Eric says ?t=448-->
