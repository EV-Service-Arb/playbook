# Effective Strategies for Recording and Verifying Google My Business Addresses

- of: [2025-12-07-effective-strategies-for-recording-and-verifying-google-my-business-addresses.md](../2025-12-07-effective-strategies-for-recording-and-verifying-google-my-business-addresses.md)
- digested by: `/ingest digest`, 2026-11-17

## gist

Eric on getting an address page video-verified at an address he does not own: three places to record (a rented coworking meeting room, the back of a mall, a rented storage unit), what to stage in each, what he sees of suspensions, and the arithmetic of why it pays. His storage-unit pages he calls near-100% on verification and about 1 in 20 suspended.

## chapters

### [00:00](https://www.loom.com/share/9a2d3dc6b2c34d07a5fe4c667a01c0a3?t=0) What address works, and the coworking room

- any commercial building works. A house has verified for him too, but "Google does typically suspend those"; his house page stayed up "a long, long time" until he changed the name, then was suspended. He does not recommend houses
  <!--Eric ran ?t=0-->
- coworking: rent a meeting or board room for an hour, about $50 (rooms from $30 and $40 to $60 an hour), one with a TV. Google looks for an office the company is headquartered in, not supplies
  <!--Eric says ?t=0-->
- stage it as the service-area video: a printed sign on the door of the room, the street address shown and held ("30 Powerhouse Street"), the door closed with his own lock glued on (a broken lock, glued) so he can film himself unlocking it with a key and walking in
  <!--Eric ran ?t=0-->
- inside, he connects his laptop by HDMI to the TV to show the brand rather than glue another sign, shows business cards, and opens manager dashboards: a Square account made by editing the page's text with inspect element, and a Go High Level dashboard; a friend in a company shirt sat looking back, "probably unnecessary, definitely overkill"
  <!--Eric ran ?t=0-->
- to the coworking staff he says he is recording a marketing video for TikTok; they "absolutely did not care", and many GMBs sit on such big commercial buildings. The page for the example, `SunCool HVAC of Toronto`, is listed as an HVAC contractor and "is still verified"
  <!--Eric ran ?t=0-->

### [06:56](https://www.loom.com/share/9a2d3dc6b2c34d07a5fe4c667a01c0a3?t=416) Mall back rooms and storage units

- free option: the back of a mall. He is 19 and finds it easier to get away with; he does not recommend it, since it means entering private areas. Permanent signage, a tour and some signs around is enough
  <!--Eric ran ?t=416-->
- best recently: a storage unit, from `Storage Mart`. Rent the cheapest, about $70-80 Canadian, an indoor facility, and cancel after because it bills monthly. Film the street sign, the address, the facility, a sign outside the unit and one inside after lifting the door; no equipment needed
  <!--Eric ran ?t=416-->
- "pretty much 100% success rate" on storage-unit pages, coworking "a little bit worse"
  <!--Eric ran ?t=416-->
- asking for a refund the same day, "I never used it", gets about half back, so a verification costs about $20, against pages that "can bring thousands of dollars every single month"
  <!--Eric says ?t=416-->

### [10:40](https://www.loom.com/share/9a2d3dc6b2c34d07a5fe4c667a01c0a3?t=640) Suspensions

- Google never says why it suspends. His early suspensions (within the first month) were mostly on the random places; the longest-lived pages are coworking ones, his first, on a large commercial building where another GMB sits. He calls it "correlation versus causation"
  <!--Eric says ?t=640-->
- storage-unit pages: "zero so far, maybe one out of like 20" suspended; the address shows no photo on Google Maps, which he thinks may be why
  <!--Eric ran ?t=640-->
- the numbers: 30 pages in a month for about two hours a day, 10 stay up, two leads a day a page is 20 leads a day; he claims 10 pages added $20-50,000 a month "easily", after ranking them
  <!--Eric says ?t=640-->
