## Conclusion

The later word stands: we start on drain cleaning or plumbing. From September on Eric puts beginners
on plumbing, and in November on drain cleaning; low ticket as the first trade is his advice through
August only, detailing by name through June. Detailing-first stays on `market/niche.md` under `### other`, as the momentum case it was
argued for; detailing itself, Eric's results on it, on `market/niches/detailing.md`. His May
"restart on a high-ticket untapped trade" stays under `### other` the same way: it is what he would do
with his experience, and it is beaten for a start by the trade paid within a day; the four trades
themselves stand as evidence that the untapped trades are mostly high ticket, each on its own page under
`market/niches/`. His February
"cleaning is as good as plumbing" is not carried: his October per-call numbers stand, and cleaning
stays off the start.

His July HVAC video does not move the start off plumbing: it puts HVAC level with plumbing, the choice
between them resting on the city's numbers, and his October list of the best fast trades has both. So
HVAC stands on `market/niche.md` as plumbing's equal where a city's numbers favour it, not as a
replacement; his November drain cleaning video names no HVAC either way.

His August recurring-trades video does not move the start either: it hands out the three trades and
their cities without calling any of them a start, and sets them against the one-off emergency trades he
runs. Its commercial cleaning is not the house cleaning the start is kept off: unlike house cleaning it
has contracts that recur and jobs that pay, so it stands on its own page,
`market/niches/commercial_cleaning.md`, and `market/niches/cleaning.md` is scoped to house cleaning. The
three stand on `market/niche.md` under recurring work, each with its own page.

What would reopen it: Eric still lists detailing among the fast trades in October, so if the owner
wants the quickest first money over the best trade to build on, detailing-first is that case; and an operator who already
runs a trade and can carry a slow close, Eric's own case in May, or who wants to run a service business rather than a marketing one, his August case, is the case for a high-ticket untapped one.
