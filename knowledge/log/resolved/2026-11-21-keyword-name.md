# keyword in the name, or the plain brand

`profile/naming.md` held two answers: Zach Peterson's "main service keyword and location in the GBP name
is a very high ranking factor", with Eric's examples of keyworded names ranking on few reviews, and a
chatgpt answer to register the plain brand and push the keywords into categories and the service area
instead, as google requires the name to match real-world use. Eric's November video names his profiles
on the biggest search. They cannot both be acted on for one profile.

## Conclusion

The keyworded name stands: the trade's biggest search and the city, as close as it goes without
sounding awkward, made to sound like a brand. Three operators who run profiles say so, and the examples
all rank on a fraction of the reviews around them; the plain-brand answer is chatgpt's reading of
google's guidelines, with no run behind it. What it guards against, a name that doesn't match the
business's real-world use, is met the way Ilya and Zach Peterson say keyworded names survive: a DBA
registered to the exact string, carried the same on the site and the paperwork, which is chatgpt's own
"alternative". So the plain brand goes under `### other`, beaten on ranking, and its reason becomes the
bullet on backing the name. The name is chosen before the profile goes live, since renaming one later
triggers re-verification.

What would reopen it: suspensions traced to keyworded names on profiles that carry a DBA to the string.
