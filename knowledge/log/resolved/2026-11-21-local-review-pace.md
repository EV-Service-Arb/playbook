# how fast reviews can go on: one a day, or forty

`reviews/rate_limits.md` holds Eric's "one review a day per profile, at most" and Ilya's review gating,
"after ~20–30 reviews in a short window google silently stops publishing new ones". Eric's November video
puts 122 reviews on a profile over a few days, ranking it within a week, and says 40 unique local
reviews can be done in a day.

## Conclusion

Scoped by where the reviews come from. Eric's one-a-day was said of bought aged gmails, and in the same
breath he made local people the exception; his November video is that exception run: local, unique
reviewers, 122 over a few days, all showing. So reviews from unique, real people go on as fast as
they can be gathered, and one a day stays the cap for the rest.

The 2026-11-19 call moves the line from local to real. Everett's 200 in two days were real students
asked in person on their own phones, some for profiles 60–120 miles off, which is not local by Eric's
rule, and they all stuck past the 1–2 week deletion delay. What the bought aged gmails behind Eric's
cap and William's loss lack is not nearness but being the reviewer's own account, which is also what
Muhammad's made-up gmails lacked. So the fast bullet now reads "real people on their own accounts" and
the cap "accounts that aren't the reviewer's own". Connor's 6-to-40 drop is kept under the cap, though
where those 40 came from is not on record. Distance itself: [review distance](2026-11-21-review-distance.md).

Ilya's gating is kept with the Newmarket profile set against it. What decides between them is not on
record: whose reviews Ilya saw gated, local or not, and on how old a profile. Neither is the member's
~60 on a new profile that got suspended. The owner's own rule in `## suspension`, a slow ramp, is
about a stale profile taking a sudden spike; the Newmarket profile was new.
