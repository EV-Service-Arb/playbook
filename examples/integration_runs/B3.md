## answer

**Short answer:** yes, the playbook records this model, usually called "rank and rent" or a retainer. The figures I found are a flat ~$500/mo for low-ticket niches and ~$2,000/mo in one plumbing example. Both are unvetted. I found no vetted market rate.

## Advice

- **Price by niche and call volume.** The only thing tying price to ticket size is Eric's lower-ticket framing [1]. Around $500/mo is the low-ticket anchor [1][4]. High-ticket trades like plumbing were pitched at ~$2,000/mo [2]. The best evidence for the top end is one unscored operator at ~$2,400/mo [4]. Treat all of these as ranges to test, since none are vetted.
- **Pitch with your own call counts.** Eric's pitch is built on the listing's average calls per month [2]. Track calls first so you can price against them.
- **Retainer or splits.** Retainers are hands-off and the income is predictable. The cost is that you give up the upside on big jobs, which Eric says commission pays more on [3]. They also suit low-ticket niches, where a per-job split yields little [1].
- **Per-lead first is a reasonable middle path.** Members suggested starting per lead and moving to a retainer once the contractor trusts the volume [5]. The Toronto retainer-plus-commission idea [6] is only a proposal.
- **Exclusivity.** Selling to one client per city is the practice described in [4]. It is also what makes a flat fee defensible, since the contractor gets all the calls.
- **Confidence.** Everything here rests on norm 0.52 or lower, and the best figure is unvetted. Don't treat $500 or $2,000 as a market price.
